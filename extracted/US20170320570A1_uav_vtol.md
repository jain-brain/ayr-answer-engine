Applicant: Aerovel-type exercise adaptation, Vancouver, BC (CA) Filed: May 9, 2016 Serial No.: 15/150,102 Patent No.: US 2017/0320570 A1 Date of Patent: Nov. 9, 2017 (Pub.) 27 Claims Classification: B64C 29/00 (2006.01); 244/17.23

## ABSTRACT

An unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), or drone, includes a fuselage 12, left and right airfoil-shaped wings 14, 16 connected to the fuselage to generate lift in forward flight, a left thrust-generating device 18 supported by the left wing, and a right thrust-generating device 20 supported by the right wing. The UAV further includes a vertical stabilizer, a top thrust-generating device 28 mounted to a top portion of the stabilizer, and a bottom thrust-generating device 34 mounted to a bottom portion. An onboard battery 38 powers the thrust-generating devices, enabling the UAV to transition between forward flight and vertical take-off and landing.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

A variety of unmanned aerial vehicles, colloquially known as drones, have been developed for military, reconnaissance and recreational uses such as aerial photography. UAVs can be gas-powered or electrically powered and can be fixed-wing or rotary-wing.

Reconnaissance UAVs are well suited to monitor or surveil remotely situated infrastructure such as oil or water pipelines, electric power lines, coastlines, farmland, borders, and forest fires. There is accordingly a need in the industry for improved unmanned aerial vehicles capable of performing extended, long-distance aerial surveillance.

The UAV 10 includes a fuselage 12 which may be made of one or more composite materials, e.g. carbon fiber, fiberglass, or a suitable metal such as aluminum. The airframe also includes left and right airfoil-shaped wings 14, 16 connected to the fuselage.

The UAV has a left thrust-generating device 18 supported by the left wing 14 and a right thrust-generating device 20 supported by the right wing 16, mechanically coupled to left and right propellers 22, 24.

The UAV has three batteries 38 symmetrically housed within the airframe: two batteries 38 are disposed within the left and right wings while a third battery 38 is disposed within the fuselage. The battery or batteries 38 may be inductively recharged via wireless power transfer (WPT) circuitry 40 while the UAV is in a tail-sitting position on a recharging pad.

An autopilot module 46 (flight controller) generates control command signals for the thrust-generating devices and control surfaces to adjust pitch, yaw and roll to keep the UAV flying within predetermined flight-envelope parameters.
